How far the indicator has moved from the baseline value to the current value.

Shaded green when the indicator moved in its good direction and red when it moved the other way; neutral indicators and unchanged values are not shaded.

Bold when larger than 10 (in either direction) and measured in % or % pts, to flag the biggest moves.
How the Delta is measured: % is a percent change, used for levels, prices and indexes; % pts (percentage points) is a simple difference, used for series that are already rates, such as an unemployment rate; Δ is a simple difference in the series' own units, used for index readings where a percent change would mislead.
Which way is good news: rising means a rise is an improvement, falling means a fall is an improvement, and neutral means the move is shown but not judged.

What each indicator means

  • Broad trade-weighted dollar: Broad trade-weighted dollar exchange rate
    Reference: [1]
  • CPI, all items: Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers: All Items in U.S. City Average
    Converted to the 12-month percent change.
    Reference: [2]
  • CPI, core (food & energy excluded): Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers: All Items Less Food and Energy in U.S. City Average
    Converted to the 12-month percent change.
    Reference: [3]
  • Chicago National Activity Index (12-mo MA): A zero value for the index indicates that the national economy is expanding at its historical trend rate of growth
    Smoothed with a trailing 12-month average.
    Reference: [4]
  • Core Personal expenditure (PCE) price index: Personal Consumption Expenditures Excluding Food and Energy Index
    Converted to the 12-month percent change.
    Reference: [5]
  • Export Price Index: Export Price Index (End Use): All Commodities Not Seasonally Adjusted
    Smoothed with a trailing 3-month average.
    Reference: [6]
  • Federal Interest Expense on Public Debt: Monthly interest expense on federal debt held outside the government
    Smoothed with a trailing 3-month average.
    Reference: [7]
  • Federal surplus/deficit: Rolling sum is mandatory: monthly federal outlays are violently seasonal.
    Smoothed with a trailing 12-month average.
    Reference: [8]
  • Goods & services trade balance: A higher trade balance indicates a surplus, which is generally considered favorable for the country.
    Smoothed with a trailing 3-month average.
    Reference: [9]
  • Import Price Index: Import Price Index (End Use): All Commodities Not Seasonally Adjusted
    Smoothed with a trailing 3-month average.
    Reference: [10]
  • Initial jobless claims: An initial claim is a claim filed by an unemployed individual after a separation from an employer
    Reference: [11]
  • Real GDP: Real gross domestic product is the inflation adjusted value of the goods and services produced by labor and property located in the United States
    Smoothed with a trailing 12-month average.
    Reference: [12]
  • Total Federal Debt: Total federal debt
    Reference: [13]
  • Total nonfinancial debt: Total nonfinancial debt
    Converted to the 12-month percent change.
    Reference: [14]
