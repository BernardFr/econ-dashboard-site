Each indicator compares its most recently available value to that of the baseline period, Oct 2023–Oct 2024. Values are computed monthly.
On a case by case basis, some indicators may have additional adjustments: e.g. 3-month averaging for highly variable series, or normalization by inflation.

The economic series being tracked. Its definition and any adjustments are described below the table.
The indicator's average over the baseline period, Oct 2023–Oct 2024, after any adjustments.
The indicator's average over the most recent months, after the same adjustments. When a series has no data yet for those months, its latest available value is used.
The unit the series is published in by its source, for example billions of dollars or an index level.
How far the indicator has moved from the baseline value to the current value.

Shaded green when the indicator moved in its good direction and red when it moved the other way; neutral indicators and unchanged values are not shaded.

Bold when larger than 10 (in either direction) and measured in % or % pts, to flag the biggest moves.
How the Delta is measured: % is a percent change, used for levels, prices and indexes; % pts (percentage points) is a simple difference, used for series that are already rates, such as an unemployment rate; Δ is a simple difference in the series' own units, used for index readings where a percent change would mislead.
Which way is good news: rising means a rise is an improvement, falling means a fall is an improvement, and neutral means the move is shown but not judged.
