How far the indicator has moved from the baseline value to the current value.

Shaded green when the indicator moved in its good direction and red when it moved the other way; neutral indicators and unchanged values are not shaded.

Bold when larger than 10 (in either direction) and measured in % or % pts, to flag the biggest moves.
How the Delta is measured: % is a percent change, used for levels, prices and indexes; % pts (percentage points) is a simple difference, used for series that are already rates, such as an unemployment rate; Δ is a simple difference in the series' own units, used for index readings where a percent change would mislead.
Which way is good news: rising means a rise is an improvement, falling means a fall is an improvement, and neutral means the move is shown but not judged.

What each indicator means

  • 10 year - 3 month Treasury Spread: Spread between 10-year and 3-month Treasury yields
    Reference: [1]
  • 10 year - 2 year Treasury Spread: Spread between 10-year and 2-year Treasury yields
    Reference: [2]
  • Delinquency rate, all bank loans: Delinquency rate, all bank loans
    Reference: [3]
  • VIX: Stock Market volatility index
    Reference: [4]
  • Effective federal funds rate: Policy anchor. Neutral because the desirable direction depends on the inflation regime.
    Reference: [5]
  • High-yield Option-Adjusted Spread: Performance of US-dollar denominated below investment grade rated corporate debt publicly issued in the US domestic market.
    Reference: [6]
  • Investment-grade Option-Adjusted Spread: Performance of US dollar denominated investment grade rated corporate debt publicly issued in the US domestic market
    Reference: [7]
  • Copper / gold price ratio: Growth vs. fear gauge. Copper: IMF monthly avg; gold: BLS export price index (first-week pricing). Level is unitless; only changes are meaningful.
    Divided by IQ12260; then smoothed with a trailing 3-month average.
    References: [8], [9]
  • Bitcoin price (Coinbase): A risk-appetite and liquidity signal: It rises when money is easy and investors are chasing speculative assets, and falls hard when financial conditions tighten
    Reference: [10]
  • Chicago Fed financial conditions: U.S. financial conditions in money markets, debt and equity markets and the traditional and "shadow" banking systems. Positive values of the NFCI indicate financial conditions that are tighter than average, while negative values indicate financial conditions that are looser than average.
    Reference: [11]
  • NFCI, adjusted for economic activity: Positive = tighter than average
    Reference: [12]
  • St. Louis Fed financial stress index: Positive = tighter than average
    Reference: [13]
  • Bank credit, all commercial banks: Bank credit, all commercial banks
    Smoothed with a trailing 3-month average.
    Reference: [14]
  • C&I lending standards (SLOOS): Percentage of Domestic Banks Tightening Standards for Commercial and Industrial Loans
    Reference: [15]
  • M2 money stock: M2 money stock
    Reference: [16]
